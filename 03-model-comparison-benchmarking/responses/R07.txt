A **virus** and a **bacterium** are both tiny microorganisms, but they are different in how they are built and how they survive.

Here are the main differences:

| Virus | Bacterium |
|---|---|
| Not considered a living organism by many scientists. | A living, single-celled organism. |
| Cannot reproduce on its own; it needs a host cell. | Can reproduce independently by dividing into two cells. |
| Much smaller than bacteria. | Generally larger than viruses. |
| Antibiotics do not work against viruses. | Some bacterial infections can be treated with antibiotics. |
| Examples: Influenza, HIV, and coronavirus. | Examples: E. coli, Salmonella, and Streptococcus. |

**In simple terms:**
- A virus is like a hijacker. It enters a living cell and uses it to make more copies of itself.
- A bacterium is a tiny living cell that can survive and reproduce on its own under suitable conditions.

**The key difference:** Bacteria are living single-celled organisms, while viruses need to enter living cells to reproduce.
