Earth has different seasons because **its axis is tilted as it travels around the Sun.**

Earth is tilted about **23.5 degrees**. This tilt causes different parts of the Earth to receive different amounts of sunlight throughout the year.

There are four main seasons:

1. **Spring:** The weather becomes warmer, and plants begin to grow.
2. **Summer:** A region receives more direct sunlight, making the days warmer and longer.
3. **Autumn (Fall):** Temperatures begin to drop, and some trees lose their leaves.
4. **Winter:** A region receives less direct sunlight, making the days colder and shorter.

### Here's an easy way to understand it:

- When your part of Earth tilts towards the Sun, you experience **summer**.
- When your part of Earth tilts away from the Sun, you experience **winter**.

**Important:** Seasons are not caused by Earth moving closer to or farther away from the Sun. They are caused mainly by Earth's tilted axis.

Also, countries near the equator, such as Nigeria, generally experience wet and dry seasons rather than four clearly defined seasons.
