**Direct Answer:**  
Generally, most plants cannot grow without *light*, but they do not strictly need *sunlight*. Furthermore, a few rare exceptions in nature and emerging scientific breakthroughs have shown that some plants can indeed grow in complete darkness.

Here is a detailed breakdown of how this works:

### 1. The General Rule: Photosynthesis Requires Light  
The vast majority of plants rely on photosynthesis to survive. This is the biological process where plants use light energy to convert carbon dioxide and water into glucose (their food) and oxygen [[1]]. Without any light, a typical plant will exhaust its stored energy reserves, become pale and spindly (a condition known as etiolation), and eventually die.

### 2. The First Exception: Artificial Light  
Plants do not specifically need sunlight; they specifically need *light energy*. Artificial grow lights that emit the correct wavelengths (primarily red and blue light) can completely replace natural sunlight for plant growth [[8]]. This principle is the foundation of modern indoor vertical farming and allows many plants to thrive in windowless environments.

### 3. The Natural Exceptions: Plants That Truly Don’t Need Light  
A small number of specialized plants have evolved to completely abandon photosynthesis and can grow in total darkness on the forest floor. They achieve this through alternative survival strategies:  
- **Mycoheterotrophic Plants**: These plants lack chlorophyll and instead parasitize mycorrhizal fungi in the soil to steal carbon and nutrients. A famous example is the Ghost Pipe (*Monotropa uniflora*), which gets all its energy from underground fungal networks [[12]]. Other examples include the Bird's-nest orchid and certain rare species of *Thismia* [[15]].  
- **Fully Parasitic Plants**: Some plants, like certain species of broomrape (*Orobanche*), attach directly to the roots of host plants and siphon off their nutrients [[10]]. Because they steal all their resources, these plants require no sunlight of their own to survive [[13]].

### 4. The Scientific Exception: Electro-Agriculture  
In a recent breakthrough, researchers have developed a method called "electro-agriculture" to grow plants without any light at all [[4]]. This process uses electrolysis to convert carbon dioxide into acetate, which is a type of salt that plants can use as a direct carbon source. Scientists have successfully engineered plants capable of thriving in total darkness by feeding off this acetate instead of relying on traditional photosynthesis [[4]].

### Summary  
While the overwhelming majority of plants require light (whether from the sun or artificial sources) to perform photosynthesis, nature has produced a few fascinating parasitic exceptions that thrive in the dark. Additionally, modern science is now actively developing ways to grow crops in complete darkness using alternative chemical energy sources.
