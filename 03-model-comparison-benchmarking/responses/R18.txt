The moon appears to change shape throughout the month because, as it orbits Earth, we see different portions of its sunlit side.

The Sun always lights up half of the Moon. However, as the Moon moves around Earth, the amount of that illuminated half we can see changes.

These changes are called **phases of the Moon**. They include:

1. **New Moon:** The Moon appears dark or nearly invisible.
2. **Waxing Crescent:** A small part of the Moon becomes visible.
3. **First Quarter:** Half of the Moon appears illuminated.
4. **Waxing Gibbous:** More than half appears illuminated.
5. **Full Moon:** The entire visible face appears bright.
6. **Waning Gibbous:** The illuminated portion begins to decrease.
7. **Third Quarter:** Half of the Moon appears illuminated again.
8. **Waning Crescent:** Only a small part remains visible before the cycle repeats.

The complete cycle takes approximately 29.5 days.

**In simple terms:** The Moon doesn't actually change shape. We just see different amounts of its sunlit side as it travels around Earth.
